Keep your home under its power limit, charge your car during the cheapest hours and use more of the solar power you produce. PELS coordinates the big loads in your home: EV chargers such as Easee and Zaptec, water heaters, heat pumps, thermostats from any brand and anything Homey can switch on and off. It works from your total consumption, read from Homey Energy, a HAN or P1 meter reader, Tibber Pulse or a Flow.

Set a limit per hour, or per quarter hour if your grid tariff counts 15-minute peaks, and a Grid import limit on the power you draw at any moment, such as your contracted power, alone or together. When you get close, PELS lowers the charging current, turns down the heating or switches devices off, lowest priority first, and brings them back as soon as there is room. A cabin, annex or rental unit with its own meter can have a limit of its own.

PELS takes prices from Homey Energy (with the grid tariff and taxes you entered there), the Power by the Hour app or a Flow. Flexible loads move to cheaper hours and temperature settings respond to the price, even with 15-minute prices, but the power limit always comes first. Smart tasks work towards a deadline: ask for the car at 80% by 07:00 or a warm living room when you get home, and PELS books the cheapest hours to get there. A simple on/off water heater can get a set amount of energy the same way. Select which cars use a charger and PELS reads the car's battery level directly, no Flow needed. In cooling mode, heat pumps and air conditioners cool harder when power is cheap or the sun is producing, and ease off when it is expensive. Home, Away and Night modes each get their own temperatures and priorities, and a Flow can switch between them when you leave or go to bed.

Got solar panels? Surplus can heat your water, preheat the house, run a device you only want on your own power, or charge the car, with the charging current following the sun. Behind an inverter limited to zero export, PELS finds the production the inverter is holding back and puts it to use. You set what exported power is worth, negative prices included, or use the feed-in price from Homey Energy. The Usage page shows production, self-consumption, export and the grid cost you avoided.

A home battery becomes part of the same plan. PELS charges it with solar surplus your devices don't use, and discharges it to keep you under the limit, by default before any device has to be turned down. If the battery is empty, PELS limits devices as usual, so the limit holds either way. This works with batteries whose Homey app lets PELS set the charge and discharge power.

A daily budget spreads your energy use over the day, and PELS can learn how the weather affects your consumption and tell you the evening before whether tomorrow fits. Dashboard widgets show available power, today's budget and Smart task progress, and let you start a task or release a held device. Flow cards bring prices, limits and tasks into your own automations.

After installing, open the Homey app and go to More > Apps > PELS > Settings. PELS shows what it needs there, step by step, and keeps your devices as they are until you switch simulation off.
