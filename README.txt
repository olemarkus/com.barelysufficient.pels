Keep your home under its power limit, charge your car in the cheapest hours and use more of your own solar power. PELS is energy management for the whole home: it controls EV chargers such as Easee and Zaptec, water heaters, heat pumps, thermostats of any brand, ventilation and anything else Homey can switch, together, based on your total consumption from Homey Energy, a HAN or P1 meter reader, Tibber Pulse or a Flow. Set a limit for the hour, or for a 15-minute average, as used by quarter-hour capacity tariffs. As you approach it, PELS lowers the charging current, turns down temperatures, or pauses and switches off devices in your priority order, and brings them back as soon as there is room. A cabin, annex or rental unit with its own meter can get its own limit.

PELS uses prices from Homey Energy, including the grid tariff and taxes you entered there, or prices from a Flow. Flexible devices run in cheaper hours, price-based temperatures follow 15-minute prices, and the power limit always comes first. Smart tasks reach a goal by a deadline, such as the car charged to 80% by 07:00, a room warm before you get home, or a set amount of energy into the water heater, and PELS books the cheapest hours before it. Tick the cars that charge at each charger, and PELS reads the car's battery level without a Flow. Heat pumps and air conditioners in cooling mode cool more in cheap hours and on solar surplus, and less when power is expensive. Modes such as Home, Away and Night give each routine its own temperatures and priorities, and a Flow can switch them when you leave or go to bed.

Have solar panels? Your surplus goes to hot water, to preheating your home, to a device that should only run on your own power, or to your car: PELS moves the charging current up and down with the sun. Even when your inverter limits export to zero, PELS can find the surplus it is holding back and put it to use. Set what your exported power is worth, even a negative price, or take the feed-in price from Homey Energy, and PELS plans with it. The Usage page shows what your panels produced, how much you used yourself, what you exported and the grid cost you avoided.

Have a home battery? PELS charges it with the solar surplus your devices do not use, and discharges it to keep you under your limit before it turns any device down. When the battery is empty, PELS limits devices as before, so your limit holds either way. This works with batteries whose Homey app accepts a charge and discharge power.

A daily budget paces energy use across the day, and PELS can learn how the weather drives your use and tell you the evening before whether tomorrow fits. Dashboard widgets show available power, today's budget and Smart task progress, and let you start a task or release a device. Flow cards let you react to prices, limits and tasks from the rest of Homey.

After installing, open the Homey app and go to More > Apps > PELS > Settings. PELS shows what it needs there, step by step, and keeps your devices as they are until you switch simulation off.
