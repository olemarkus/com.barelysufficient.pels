# Home battery discharge order

Proposed for owner decision. The code references describe main at `69412418a`.

## Problem

An owner wanted spare solar to charge the home battery first and the car second. That means putting the battery above the car in Settings → Modes. During a peak, the same order turns the car down before an eligible full battery is asked to supply it. Putting the battery last fixes the peak behaviour and gives the car first use of the sun.

Charging order expresses who should get scarce solar first. Discharge spends stored energy to avoid a cost. Turning a device down can cost comfort, time or a missed deadline. Discharging incurs losses and wear and leaves less for a later expensive hour or for backup. A single position currently makes both decisions.

## Proposed behaviour

**Spare solar follows Modes.** Keep the ranked pool, including charge surrendered to a waiting device above the battery. Keep economic planning in its existing order. The battery's position still has a concrete effect on when it gets spare solar.

**Use available discharge first for physical limits.** For capacity pace, a projected hard-cap breach or a spent period, give eligible battery discharge first use of the physical relief budget. Apply the same policy for grid import and per-phase import protection. Keep a battery's allocated spare-solar charge at its Modes turn, even during a limit. Otherwise, a battery above the car could lose its solar while the car runs on the room that frees. Cut demonstrably grid-funded charging first. The early cut may spend only the charge above the battery's ranked solar share. The allocator's total reservation can also contain planned grid charge, so use the ranked solar offer and the resolved charge decision to establish that share. Keep unclassified charge in the ranked turn and credit stopped charging only under the existing observation rules. Then offer discharge from batteries that are already supplying or can start without taking away an allocated solar charge. If the ranked walk reaches a solar-charging battery, cap its charge there. It can cross zero to supply a remaining physical need when the same bounds allow it. Apply `compareLoads` to loads between the remaining ranked charge turns. Available-power relays and preemptive step-downs can rank across old boundaries for batteries with no remaining charge turn. Leave phase-export protection and the silent-meter directive under their existing rules. This policy allocates relief before deciding which loads to cut. Writes and delivery are asynchronous. **Use one physical relief budget.** Resolve the largest enabled capacity-pace, projected-cap and grid deficit independently of the daily pace and the binding label. The daily remainder waits for the next meter reading, one reading later than today's ordinary capacity-pace path. Repeated builds of the same sample cannot spend the remainder as additional physical relief. **Spent period and grace.** Spend the finite `import_target` under the physical ordering above and limit loads for any residual. Preserve the grid and urgent bypasses, same-sample handling and the existing margin needed to fund a wait.

**Resume in the existing Modes order.** Treat easing a discharge and resuming regular operation as two decisions. The storage stage eases negative limit holds against physical capacity, grid and phase room, including the spent-period target and the measured consequences of other batteries backing off. The restore pass then considers the managed-idle transition in the existing Modes order, with existing headroom, settle, batch, reservation and waiting rules. A charge cap may remain while budget pressure blocks routine resumption. Once admitted, the next measured cycle can follow ranked solar or the continuous plan. Device admission continues to withhold observed or held discharge and unmeasured charge increases. A limited device may have to wait for genuine room as discharge eases, even if the meter shows room created by a battery. Where real room already exists, ordinary admission can still resume a device before every battery reaches idle. There is no mirrored walk: restore uses its own order, preconditions and ledger, and shares only a defensive ID tie-breaker with the current scalar priority sort. Keep the existing safety and opt-out releases separate from managed idle. ### Later: limit loads that can wait at no cost

Have the owning temperature and smart-task domains resolve whether a specific reduction can be recovered without losing comfort or missing a goal. A heater needs trusted temperature above its limited target, enough margin to coast through the proposed pause and room to recover. A smart task needs time and feasible power after higher-priority commitments, with any restart and settling costs included. An above-target reading or a distant deadline alone proves neither. A time-bounded, recoverable reduction could precede battery discharge, using existing Modes order among eligible loads and re-evaluating on measured cycles. A lost or expired guarantee would return the load to normal selection. An urgent cycle must still bank only electrical relief. A temperature request alone cannot delay a battery on the strength of a promised reduction. Design the semantic input and cost calculation separately. The first change needs no new planner hook, vendor field, owner setting or dependency on task scheduling. ## Alternatives considered

| Option | Trade-off |
| --- | --- |
| Keep today's behaviour and explain it better | Preserves the ability to spare battery energy by placing a pool pump below it. Clearer wording still leaves the owner unable to give the battery solar first and protect the car during a peak. |
| Add a separate discharge position | Can express the pool-pump preference independently. Adds another per-mode order, another default for existing and newly discovered devices, more UI and settings ownership, and keeps cost encoded as a static rank. |
| Always protect everything, including during daily pacing | One visible rule and fewer soft interruptions. A very low budget pace could drain the battery even when the home is comfortably below its physical limits. It can spend energy intended for a later hour and still require buying it back. |
| Keep today's discharge order for daily pace or move deferrable loads first | Keeping it preserves more transitions but spends energy based on solar rank. Deferrable-first can be an improvement once the owner domains can prove recoverability. The initial budget policy needs neither assumption. |

## Migration and risk

Keep the saved `capacity_priorities` exactly as entered, including the default placement and persisted ranks. The same order still expresses solar preference, scheduled fleet order, resumption and the priority of charge cuts under daily-only pressure. Keep `controllable_devices`, `battery_control_devices`, `continuous_battery_plan` and `battery_control_claim.<deviceId>` under their current owners and formats. The proposal needs zero settings writes at upgrade and zero public contract changes. Any later public contract extension must be additive. The settings owner rules already distinguish genuine absence from failed reads and retain accepted state, so there is no reason to rewrite a catalog or add a persisted migration marker. [settings-key-ownership.md:161](settings-key-ownership.md#L161) · [settings-key-ownership.md:182](settings-key-ownership.md#L182) · [settings-key-ownership.md:202](settings-key-ownership.md#L202) · [settings-key-ownership.md:316](settings-key-ownership.md#L316) · [persisted-settings-state.md:3](persisted-settings-state.md#L3)

An owner who put a pool pump below the battery to avoid spending stored energy on it will see the battery help hold the physical limit while the pump keeps running, up to the battery's available relief. That can increase discharge and reduce energy available for a later expensive hour. The single-battery physical-limit order stays familiar for a battery already last that can deliver bounded power. Homes can also see different recovery timing as battery discharge eases before it can be replaced by genuine room for a device. A meter-following battery could lose its live discharge turn if its control surface cannot provide an enforceable bound. Homes that previously used stored energy for daily-only pressure may see devices limited sooner. Explain both changes in the release text and the updated in-product hint. Existing reserve and Power-limit control still apply, and the latter turns off both solar steering and limit help for that battery.

The safety-sensitive transitions are mixed pressure, a period already spent, multiple storage commands from one sample, and recovery while an old setpoint still appears in the meter. The regression cases above should prove bounded credits, no discharge-funded admission or solar starts, fall-through on non-delivery, and no rapid sign changes under stable conditions. Vendor-specific behaviour stays in the device adapters. The planner uses the existing resolved device facts and writes remain behind the actuator.

## Open questions for the owner

1. **Which physical limits get the battery-first turn?** Apply it to Capacity limit (including capacity pace and spent periods), Grid import limit and eligible main-fuse import relief. Keep phase-export and meter-outage behaviour under their existing rules.
2. **Can a scheduled hold or EV pause veto physical relief?** Keep today's override. The reserve and device bounds hold, and the EV switch governs scheduled discharge and sales.
3. **What should the daily-budget path do?** Leave incremental discharge to the continuous plan, cap charging in Modes order when needed, and reconsider daily pressure on a fresh reading after sizing a simultaneous physical response.
4. **Which battery supplies the next watt, and who resumes first?** Prefer an eligible battery already supplying, with a limit hold first and stable ID as the final tie-breaker. Ease the last battery called on first, then keep ranked resumption to managed idle once real room is available. Use a shared live bound. Revisit wear balancing if concentrated use warrants it.
5. **How should we handle intentional mid-list placements?** Keep the saved rank and announce the behaviour change. Use a separate follow-up to design recoverable low-cost pauses. Add a per-device exception only if the owner wants to preserve explicit protection choices after that design is considered.
6. **When should a meter-following battery get a live discharge turn?** Require the device owner to establish a controllable bound for the physical need, phase room and reserve. Leave the battery out of incremental live discharge when that bound cannot be enforced.
7. **When should a battery already charging from spare solar give up that charge?** Keep its solar charge at its Modes turn, even during physical limiting. Give charge drawn from the grid and available discharge the first turn. This preserves the reason an owner put the battery above the car.
